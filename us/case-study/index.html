Case study · 30 independent auto repair shops

What $1,000 a month in Google Ads brings an independent repair shop

We looked at the first 30 days after a 2-week setup phase across 30 independent repair shops in Europe. On average, $1,000 in ad spend brought in 29 leads at about $35 each, and more than half of them were written quote requests.

Four things every account gets in the first two weeks

One page per job

A driver searching for a timing belt price lands on a timing belt page with a real price, not on the home page. Brakes, AC, transmissions and scheduled maintenance each get their own page, and every ad group points to the page that matches.

Every lead counted

Quote forms, calls from the website and taps on the ad's call button are all tracked. Every request lands in a lead sheet the owner can check from a phone.

Ads that stay on

Drivers send quote requests early in the morning, in the evening and on weekends. The ads keep running while many competing shops have theirs switched off.

No paying for the wrong clicks

Searches for other shops' names, DIY questions and parts shopping are excluded, so the budget goes to people looking for a repair.

What this means in Florida

  • Florida drivers search "auto repair near me" about 27,000 times a month and "car AC repair near me" almost 10,000 times.
  • Top-of-page clicks for those searches cost roughly $2–10, in the same range as the shops in this group.
  • The work is the same: a page for every job, honest prices, ads that run when people search, and every call counted.

How we measured. Averages across 30 independent auto repair shops in Europe, taken from each shop's Google Ads account and lead records for the first 30 days after a 2-week setup phase. Non-brand search campaigns only. A lead is a written quote request from an ad click, a call from the website, or a tap on the ad's call button. Figures are rounded and shown in US dollars. Search volumes and click prices are Google Keyword Planner estimates for Florida.
