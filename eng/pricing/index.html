The cost of Google Ads always has two separate parts: the ad budget, which you pay directly to Google, and the management fee for whoever builds and runs the campaigns. This page covers both and explains how to choose the right level for your business.

What makes up the price of Google Ads?

1. The ad budget paid to Google

Google Ads is click-based: you only pay when someone clicks your ad. The click price is set in an auction based on how many other companies want the same search and how good your ad and page are. In Finland a click typically costs €1–5 in most service industries, and more in the most competitive ones.

The ad budget is charged directly to your own Google Ads account. It does not go through our invoicing, so you always see exactly where the money went.

2. The management fee

The management fee covers building the campaigns, maintaining keywords and ads, cutting out wasted searches, tracking and reporting. A well-managed account gets clearly more enquiries from the same budget than one left on its own, so management pays for itself in the clicks it saves.

Why is the minimum ad budget €500/month?

Google Ads learns from data. If a click costs €3, a €500 budget gives you about 170 clicks a month. That is enough when you advertise one or two services. We still recommend starting at €1,000, which gives about 330 clicks, shows sooner which searches bring enquiries and which don't, and lets Google's automatic bidding work.

With a budget below €500, so few clicks accumulate that a single good or bad week distorts the whole picture. The management fee then becomes too large a share of the total cost, and nobody can tell whether advertising pays off. That is why we don't take on budgets below €500.

Example calculation: a €1,000 budget, an average click price of €3 and 6% of clickers getting in touch gives about 20 enquiries a month, roughly €65 per enquiry including management. The numbers vary a lot by industry, and we make a precise estimate in the free consultation.

What does the Complete package mean in practice?

Complete is the broadest package. For example, on a service company's account with a monthly budget of about €4,000, it has meant:

  • taking the old account apart and building seven new campaigns by service, area and audience, plus a separate brand campaign
  • shared negative keyword lists for competitor names, services the company doesn't sell and towns it doesn't serve
  • moving conversion tracking into the client's own Tag Manager and Google Analytics, so that calls, emails, quote requests and bookings are each measured once
  • choosing the right landing page for every campaign and adjusting the ad copy to match
  • reviewing search terms and bids weekly, with a report every two weeks
